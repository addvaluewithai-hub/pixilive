# Character Engine architecture

## Ownership

React owns the interface and selected character. The SVG adapter owns the renderer lifecycle. The performance director owns expression and gesture arbitration. The playback clock owns audio scheduling and mouth samples. Gemini requests semantic performance cues; it never writes SVG coordinates.

The director samples against `AudioContext.currentTime` during Live playback and a monotonic clock during offline demonstrations. Animation geometry is updated locally per frame. A switch disposes the old rig and cancels its pending performance while the PCM stream and conversation continue.

## Timing boundary

Gemini cues have no authoritative alignment to their intended words in this implementation. They are held until the next received audio chunk and anchored to that chunk's scheduled playback start. Cues without a following audio chunk are dropped at turn completion. This is best-effort phrase timing, not word-level synchronization. A production-quality semantic alignment layer needs a measured protocol or alignment source and a latency budget.

Mouth samples are generated by the inherited acoustic analyzer and sampled on the playback clock. The transcript-letter timing guide is intentionally not fed into the Live path. The analyzer is a heuristic fallback; it is not an Arabic phoneme recognizer and cannot promise every consonant is classified correctly. The mouth renderer supports 12 visemes, including closure, teeth and tongue poses, independently of detection quality. REST/MBP closure uses faster settling than other channels.

`turnComplete` means generation finished. Listening only resumes after scheduled playback drains. Interruption clears both queued sound and visual cues. A client-side interrupt suppresses remaining output until server interruption or turn completion. Reconnection may resume an existing session; explicit close clears the handle.

## Reuse and isolation

- `public/character-engine`: our Character Lab master and shared geometry/rig, extended with direct mouth, intensity, cancellation and independent gesture APIs.
- `VisemeAnalyzer.ts`: acoustic fallback adapted from PixiLive main (`d85f0d5`). Its limitations are retained and documented rather than presented as precise lip sync.
- `audio-capture.worklet.js`: the reviewed capture processor from main.
- New application, director, playback clock, resampler lifecycle, and Gemini adapter do not depend on old Pixi/Rive character classes or iframe/button bridges.

## Remaining real-device validation

1. Verify token issuance and Live tool support against the configured Gemini project.
2. Exercise Arabic speech, silence, fast phrases, laughter, and interruptions on desktop and mobile.
3. Measure perceived timing including output-device latency, stalls, suspended audio contexts and network jitter.
4. Review arms/tails while switching gestures and switching characters mid-speech.
5. Before exposing the token endpoint publicly, apply the application's authentication and rate limits at the server/edge. The endpoint currently checks request origin, constrains tokens, uses one-use short-lived credentials, and avoids returning upstream secrets; origin checks are not user authentication.
